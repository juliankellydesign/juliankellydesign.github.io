Lightning Bolt

One red mana, 3 damage, any target. What if it dealt 4? What if it dealt 5?

Lightning Bolt has been the benchmark for red removal since 1993. Three damage for one mana is already efficient. But efficiency is abstract until you ask what it actually kills — specifically, the creature commanders people actually play.

So I pulled the top 1,000 commanders from EDHREC (Past 2 Years) and looked up the toughness of each one. Of the 1,000, 985 are creatures on the front face; the other 15 are planeswalkers. Here is how the full 1,000 distribute:

The distribution peaks at exactly 4 toughness (262 commanders), with 3 right behind it (257). Real Lightning Bolt, at 3 damage, answers 459 of the 985 creature commanders — 46.6%. Useful, but you feel the ceiling.

One more damage changes everything. Going from 3 to 4 steps over the single largest toughness bucket in the format:

73.2%
die if Bolt dealt 4 · 721 / 985 creature commanders
89.0%
die if Bolt dealt 5 · 877 / 985 creature commanders

If Bolt dealt 4, a one-mana instant would kill nearly three out of four of the most-played creature commanders in the format, on sight, at instant speed. That card would already be very, very good — premium removal in any deck that can cast it.

If Bolt dealt 5, the argument is over. Nearly nine in ten creature commanders die to it, and the survivors are mostly things like The Ur-Dragon that you were never bolting anyway. A one-mana “destroy almost any commander” is not a card you consider — it is an auto-include in every deck that produces a red mana.

Two footnotes. The 15 planeswalker commanders aren’t toughness-killed at all (damage removes loyalty, not life), so they’re out of the percentages entirely. The 7 commanders with * toughness stay in the denominator but never count as a fixed kill — their toughness depends on the board.

Data: EDHREC Past 2 Years top 1,000 commanders, snapshot 2026-09-24. Toughness is front-face, via Scryfall. Kill percentages are out of the 985 creature commanders (the 7 with * toughness included, never counted as killed).
